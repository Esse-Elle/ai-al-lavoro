Chapter six, part two. An example of how they work together: analysing a trade fair. You ask Claude: "analyse the September fair". Claude coordinates three agents working in parallel. A photo agent analyses the stands, with its visual reading skill. A press agent reads articles and social media, with the press review skill. And a strategy agent draws the conclusions, using the tone of voice skill and the PowerPoint report skill. Each at its own desk, each with its own method.

Then there are connectors. They connect Claude to your tools: mail, calendar, Drive, SharePoint, Canva, the CRM. Behind them is a standard called MCP: think of it as a USB-C socket, one shape for lots of different devices. You turn them on from Customize, Connectors, and Claude only sees what you authorise. And there's Claude in Chrome, which browses websites for you. One clarification that often comes in handy: you don't need a connector to create a PowerPoint or an Excel file. That's something Claude can do on its own. A connector is for reading from and writing to external systems.

Scheduled tasks are the collaborator that works even when you're not there. You describe a job once and Claude repeats it on its own: every hour, every day, every week or on demand. They run in the cloud, even with your computer switched off. You create them from Scheduled, with "Create with Claude": you describe the job in words and Claude asks you the right questions. They use the connectors and skills you've already turned on, and they're the foundation of all the automations we'll see shortly.

And then Claude Code. It's the mode where Claude writes and runs code, and you'll find it in the Code tab of the desktop app. It was born for developers, but today it's useful for non-programmers too. A few examples for marketing: renaming five hundred photos from a shoot in one go, creating a mini site for an event, turning a price list into product sheets, editing and cutting a video, building small custom tools. When is it better than the chat? When the work touches dozens or hundreds of files, when you need a tool to reuse rather than an answer, or when you want a technical result like a web page. You open the Code tab, choose the folder, describe the result, not the code, and try it on a few files before running it on all of them.

A final word on security. When Claude acts on mail, files and tools, you decide how much autonomy to give it. Manual, the default, means Claude asks before acting: that's the starting point. Auto means it works without asking at every step, and should only be used for drafts and research, when the workflow is proven. Irreversible actions, meaning sending, paying, deleting and publishing, always stay with your approval. Connect only the folders and accounts you need, and review your active connectors and skills from time to time.

Let's see it all together in a second pipeline: from a product launch to all the content. First, the brand project, with tone of voice and the product sheet for the new item in the folder. Second, Claude writes the press release, for example two thousand five hundred characters maximum, with a quote from the marketing director, and creates it as a Doc. Third, you review and approve it. Fourth, from the approved release Claude derives three posts for Instagram and LinkedIn and a newsletter. Fifth, with Claude Design it creates the poster for the showroom event, in the brand colours. One single context, many formats.
