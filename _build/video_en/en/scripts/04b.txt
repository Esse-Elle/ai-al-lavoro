Chapter four, part two. So how does Claude work when you hand it a task? It always follows the same cycle. It understands: it reads the request and, if needed, asks you questions. It plans: it breaks the work down into steps, which you see scrolling by. It uses tools: it reads files, searches the web, uses connectors. It asks: for important actions it waits for your go-ahead. And it delivers: it saves files or artifacts and sums up what it did. You always stay in control: you can stop it, correct it, ask it to redo a step.

So before you start, make three choices. Permissions: Manual, the default, to begin with. Claude asks before it acts. Auto lets it work on its own, with automatic safety checks, but you should only use it when the workflow is proven. Where it works: a dedicated folder, never the whole disk. And with what: only the connectors and skills that this job needs.

Now a topic that will save you time and money: models and tokens. The model is the brain that answers. Anthropic offers several, like engines of different power. Haiku is quick and light, for simple tasks. Sonnet is balanced, the everyday model. Opus is for deep reasoning, for serious work. Fable is designed for long projects to be carried forward on its own. The number next to the name, for example five point five, is the generation: the higher it is, the more recent. And next to the model there's the effort, meaning how much Claude thinks before answering.

A token, on the other hand, is the unit an AI reads and writes in: a piece of a word, about three or four characters. Everything is measured in tokens: what you write, attachments, internal reasoning and the answer. And above all: with every message, Claude rereads the whole conversation, attachments included. Think about it: the eighth message in a chat costs as much as all the previous ones put together. An endless chat costs more and more, and sooner or later it makes you hit your plan's limit, which resets every five hours, plus a weekly cap. The same goes for models: the more powerful the model and the higher the effort, the more tokens each answer uses. Haiku uses little, Sonnet a medium amount, Opus a lot, Fable a huge amount. Using Opus to summarise an email is like doing the grocery shopping in a Formula One car.

So how do you choose? With four questions. Is it simple and quick? Haiku: summarise, extract, answer briefly. Is it everyday work? Sonnet: texts, analysis, plans. Do I need to think it through? Opus: strategies, complex documents. Is it long and autonomous? Fable. And the practical rule: start with Sonnet, and only move up a model if the answer isn't good enough.

Seven habits for using Claude mindfully. Choose a model and effort that fit the task. One chat per topic: when you change subject, open a new one. Don't upload the same files again: use projects and folders. Group your questions into a single message. Ask for short answers when a short answer is enough. Only use deep research when you really need it. And every now and then, check your usage in the settings.
